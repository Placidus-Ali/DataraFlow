Key Points

The Challenge:
The DataraFlow Founder Challenge 2026 (The Niagara Succession Signal) is a prize-backed research 
competition running alongside Cohort 2 of the DataraFlow Data Science Mentorship Programme.


Core Problem:
You must analyze small-business succession risk in Ontario's Niagara Region (Canada) to identify businesses 
at risk of closure due to retiring owners without succession plans, estimate exposed jobs, map risks by sector 
and municipality, and propose remediation strategies.


Prize & Timeline:
₦500,000 awarded to the winning individual or team. The challenge runs from Month 1 through Month 5 of the internship,
with final submissions due at the start of Month 6.


Eligibility & Teams:
Open to enrolled DataraFlow Cohort 2 interns, competing individually or in teams of up to 4 members.


Regulatory Guardrail:
Your work must strictly remain aggregated economic research and generic frameworks; you are prohibited 
from providing financial, tax, or sale advice to specific, identifiable businesses.


Key Evaluation Criteria:
Submissions are judged on Analytical Rigour & Method (25%):

1. What Is Expected: The panel looks for a sound analytics, correct handling and cleaning of datasets, 
defensible modeling choices, and strong domain alignment with economic research standards.

2. Reproducibility & Statistical Reporting (20%): 
What Is Expected: An outsider who is not part of the team should be able to clone your repository, run your scripts, 
and obtain the exact same output, metrics, and dataset without manual interventions. It also requires honest 
reporting of model uncertainty and statistical assumptions.
 
3. Asset Completeness (15%):
What Is Expected: Every single required deliverable outlined in the challenge guidelines must be present, 
and well-aligned with the rest of your package. Missing core deliverables will directly penalize this score.

4. Insight & Real-World Usefulness (15%):
What Is Expected: Findings and recommendations must provide actionable value to actual regional decision-makers 
(such as municipal economic development departments, chambers of commerce, and buyers) rather than remaining purely 
theoretical.

5. Visual Communication (15%), and Innovation (10%):
What Is Expected: Clear, publication-ready visual assets that translate complex analytical findings into accessible 
insights for non-technical stakeholders and executive readers.



Required Submission Deliverables

To enter a complete submission package at the start of Month 6, you must submit the following items:

Research Report:
The central structured report detailing your methodology, findings, and evidence-based recommendations (PDF format).


Aggregated Dataset:
Cleaned, documented dataset merging core open sources (Canadian Business Counts, LEAP Business Dynamics, NEI data, 
Census profiles) at the sector and municipal level (CSV or Parquet format).


Reproducibility & Code Bundle:
A Git repository link or zipped bundle containing scripts (Python/R), Jupyter/Quarto notebooks, fixed random seeds, 
environment setup, and a README for complete replication.


Infographics & Standalone Visual Assets:
Non-technical graphics summarizing headline findings (risk maps, exposed job counts, municipal breakdowns).


Graphs & Exploratory Visuals:
Analytical charts backing up claims, including data distributions, trend correlations, and model diagnostics.


Predictive Forecasting Model:
Fully documented model files predicting business closures or at-risk counts with validated metrics, 
baseline comparisons, and clear uncertainty estimates.


Generic Succession / Acquisition Playbook:
A transferable guide mapping out transition sequences, buyer signals, and failure points for policymakers or 
prospective buyers.


Local-Economy Impact Analysis:
Aggregate breakdown evaluating sectoral concentration, job exposure, municipal vulnerabilities, and economic 
ripple effects.


Remediation Report:
A decision-ready briefing featuring targeted action items split separately for government bodies and private-sector 
actors.


Originality Declaration:
A brief signed statement confirming original work, adherence to data ethics standards, and proper source licensing.


Optional Extension Assets (Bonus/Innovation):
Any attempted extras such as interactive dashboards, succession-readiness scoring indices, synthetic 
buyer-matching models, policy briefs, or African regional comparative case studies.
